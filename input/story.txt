Thirteen-year-old Stacy loved metal detecting, a hobby she had learned from her grandfather. One day, when the beach was closed due to strong winds, she followed his old advice and went searching in the forest near a large oak tree. After finding a few small metal objects and bullet shells, her detector signaled again. This time, she uncovered a large silver coin engraved with an unusual crest. Excited, she took photos and shared her discovery on her small social media page.
Within hours, the post went viral. Among the many comments, one anonymous user urgently offered to buy the coin and asked her to delete the post. Shortly afterward, the photo mysteriously disappeared. The next morning, Stacy woke up to police cars surrounding her house. Officers took her and her mother to the station for questioning.

The coin, investigators explained, had recently been reported stolen from a private collection. After hearing Stacy’s account and confirming the discovery site, authorities determined she had found it legitimately. She was cleared of suspicion and later received a finder’s reward.

The experience taught Stacy an important lesson about responsibility and caution, especially when sharing valuable discoveries online.
